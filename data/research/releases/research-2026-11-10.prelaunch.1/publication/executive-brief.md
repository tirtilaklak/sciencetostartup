## 1\. Delivery unit before provider

In this reviewed sample, a useful supplier comparison begins with the delivery unit: participant access, licensed data, custom tasks or executable environments\.

Buyer implication: Specify who owns task design, annotation, QA, rights clearance and acceptance before comparing proposals\.

Confidence: Moderate for this sample’s disclosed distinctions; low for prevalence outside it\.

Limits: First-party offering descriptions do not establish interchangeable services, capacity, realized quality or a market-wide supplier ranking\.

## 2\. Human and model provenance

Human content, human labels and human oversight are distinct provenance claims; the selected datasets contain materially different combinations\.

Buyer implication: Require a stage-level provenance manifest and test the label target relevant to the intended task\.

Confidence: High for the documented provenance distinctions; unestablished for comparative training outcomes\.

Limits: The reviewed methods are source-reported and version-specific\. No universal advantage of human or synthetic labels is established\.

## 3\. Agreement needs denominators

Released agreement and preference-pair totals cannot be interpreted without the independent-judgment and exclusion denominators\.

Buyer implication: Request raw-to-retained counts, invalid-item rules, repeat labels, disagreement slices and corrected acceptance\.

Confidence: High for the methodological distinction; low for transferring a numerical agreement result between workflows\.

Limits: The reviewed workflows differ in task and sampling\. They cannot rank provider quality, and no common reliability measurement was executed\.

## 4\. Artifact role is narrow

Named artifact attribution is strongest when it preserves the exact contribution stage and release rather than extending a brand association across a family\.

Buyer implication: Ask for source-supported roles for the specific delivered artifact; keep recruitment, annotation, validation and authorship separate\.

Confidence: High for the explicit scoped roles in this sample; incomplete for the broader supplier graph\.

Limits: These examples do not reveal contract values, contribution shares, exhaustive supplier lists or causal provider performance\.

## 5\. Benchmark protocol before score

Benchmark family names alone are insufficient for comparing scores or deciding deployment readiness\.

Buyer implication: Freeze task version, candidate responses, aggregation and execution environment, then inspect errors on the buyer’s actual task\.

Confidence: High for protocol differences and stated limitations; unestablished for any new system’s performance\.

Limits: No benchmark was executed for this report\. The reviewed sources do not establish a universal deployment-quality ranking\.

## 6\. Commercial denominators stay separate

The selected commercial disclosures cannot be combined into supplier market shares, production-capacity rankings or a common unit-cost comparison\.

Buyer implication: Retain period, currency, organizational scope, active-window definition and acceptance basis; obtain a project-specific quote and availability check\.

Confidence: High for the incompatibility of these disclosed denominators; no confidence claim is made about market-wide shares\.

Limits: Coverage is selected and incomplete\. Missing disclosure is unknown, not zero; the observed figures are not matched measures\.

## 7\. Multilingual intersections before language counts

Multilingual suitability depends on language-domain-task intersections and native validity, rather than a language count alone\.

Buyer implication: Specify every required intersection, preserve translation/review provenance and calibrate the judge in the target locale\.

Confidence: Moderate for these documented methodological distinctions; unmeasured for current supplier performance\.

Limits: These selected studies do not establish universal translation inferiority, global language coverage or a provider ranking\.

## 8\. Rights follow exact material and service

Rights, privacy and security diligence must follow exact components, services and uses rather than a top-level policy or license label\.

Buyer implication: Obtain component rights and the actual project processing/security scope before accepting data or environments\.

Confidence: High for the documented scope differences; unestablished for legal compliance of particular deployments\.

Limits: This is public-source evidence and methodological judgment, not legal advice, rights clearance or an assurance audit\.

## 9\. Verifiable outcomes need side effect review

Executable success checks should be paired with independent review of constraints, side effects and verifier error\.

Buyer implication: Test valid alternatives, plausible shortcuts, harmful extra actions and infrastructure failures in the acceptance pilot\.

Confidence: High for the stated benchmark limitations; unmeasured for any new buyer deployment\.

Limits: No environment or provider was independently executed for this report, and no failure-prevalence estimate is claimed\.
